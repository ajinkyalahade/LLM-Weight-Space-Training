Part A · Understand the edit
1

Read the blog post and keep a copy

Done

Researchers showed that an AI can open another model's weight file, find what makes it misbehave, and fix it by hand.

  • Post: "Sparks of Weight-Space Programming", MIT CSAIL, 2 Nov 2026. Saved offline in this folder.
  • Their headline: editing 0.016% of Qwen3.5-0.8B's weights stopped its loops. Math score went from 7.9% to 48.1%.
  • Their code and logs are public (MIT / CC BY 4.0). I downloaded them as a reference.

Done

Out of 488 blocks of numbers, only 2 changed. In each, 64 "neurons" had their push in one direction reversed and made 5× stronger.

I watched the model's insides while it was still reasoning, then again once it was looping, and took the difference. That points at the same spot the authors found.

How I knew which numbers to change

Watch the model get stuck. Compare that with the times it doesn't. Find the neurons that push it toward stuck, and reverse them. This is the method behind edit M2, which scored 39.1% on 64 new problems, against 32.8% for the authors' edit.

1

Collect stuck moments

Take answers where the original model fell into a loop, and mark where each loop starts.

  • 40 looping answers from the original model's run on 96 problems.
  • A loop starts where the text begins repeating word for word. The median start was about 7,000 tokens in.

4

Find which neurons push that way

Each neuron adds its own column of 1,024 numbers to the inner state whenever it fires. For each one, measure how far it pushes along the "stuck" direction during real stuck moments, then keep the 64 strongest.

Layer 19: push toward "stuck", strongest 24 of 3,584 neurons (real values)

Neuron numbers along the bottom. Two neurons, #11 and #1, do most of the pushing. Another 40 with small pushes were also chosen, and the other 3,520 were left alone. Layer 15 is done the same way.

Flip them

In each chosen neuron's column, reverse its push along the "stuck" direction and make it 5× stronger. The rest of the column stays as it was.
